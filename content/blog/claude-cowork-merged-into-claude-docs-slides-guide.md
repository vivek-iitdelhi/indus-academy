If you'd started using Claude Cowork for longer tasks — a report that needed research, a spreadsheet that needed cleaning up, a project you handed off and checked on later — that separate mode is gone. On September 16, 2026, Anthropic [merged Cowork back into regular Claude chat](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-is-now-claude) and added two new features: Claude Docs and Claude Slides. If you use Claude for work, this changes how you should be working with it.

## What actually changed

Before this update, Claude had three ways to work: a normal chat, Claude Cowork for longer, file-heavy or multi-step tasks, and Claude Code for programming. Now there are two: chat and code. You no longer pick a mode before you start — you just describe what you need in a normal conversation, and Claude decides whether it's a quick answer or a longer piece of work it should hand off and keep running on.

The practical effect: you can ask a question, then in the same thread ask Claude to take on an extended task — say, going through a folder of vendor contracts — and it keeps working even after you close your laptop, the way Cowork did. You don't need a separate app or tab for that anymore.

If you were already using Cowork, your existing tasks, projects, connectors, custom skills, artifacts and files carry over and open the way they did before, so nothing you built there is lost.

## The two new pieces: Claude Docs and Claude Slides

**Claude Docs** lets you ask for a document directly inside a chat, and Claude builds it in front of you rather than dropping a wall of text you have to copy out. It behaves a lot like a shared Google Doc: it starts private to you, you can invite colleagues to edit it, several people (and Claude) can work on it at the same time, and comments attach to specific lines of text the way you'd expect.

**Claude Slides** works the same way for presentations. You describe the deck you need — audience, number of slides, key points — and Claude drafts it. You can then edit slides directly yourself or tell Claude what to change ("make slide 4 a comparison table," "shorten the intro"). When it's ready, you can present straight from Claude, share it by link, or download it as a PowerPoint or PDF file to send on.

Claude Design, Anthropic's tool for visual layouts, now works inside ordinary conversations too, instead of being a separate destination.

Both Docs and Slides are in beta and currently limited to paid plans.

## Who gets it and when

The rollout is staged:

- **Pro and Max plans** get it first, across web, desktop and mobile, over the coming weeks.
- **Team and Free plans** are next, though Anthropic hasn't given an exact date.
- **Enterprise** admins get at least 30 days' notice before it reaches their organization, and there's nothing they need to configure to turn it on when it arrives.

If you're on a paid personal plan and don't see it yet, it's a staged rollout, not a sign anything is broken.

## How to actually use this at work this week

1. **Stop building documents by copy-pasting Claude's chat output into Word.** If you have Docs, ask directly: "Write this as a document I can edit and share with my team." You save the export step and get a shareable link instead of a static file.
2. **Draft your next internal presentation in one pass.** Give Claude the audience, the three or four points you need to make, and any data to reference, then ask for a first draft deck. Expect to spend your time editing and tightening rather than building slides from a blank template.
3. **Use one thread instead of switching tools.** If a conversation starts as a quick question and turns into something bigger — say, you're asking about a vendor's contract terms and it turns into reviewing all ten of your vendor contracts — you can now ask Claude to take that on in the same thread instead of starting over in a separate Cowork-style mode.
4. **Treat drafts as drafts.** Docs and Slides speed up the first version. Client-facing numbers, contractual language and anything with legal or financial weight still need a human check before they go out — the tool writing faster doesn't mean you should verify less.
5. **If you manage a Claude Enterprise account,** flag the 30-day notice window to your IT or security team now, so document- and deck-sharing permissions are decided before Docs and Slides show up for your users rather than after.

## Why this matters beyond this week

The direction is clear: general AI assistants are becoming full workspaces — chat, documents, slides, delegated tasks — inside one product, not a bundle of separate tools you have to choose between. Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 have Gemini and Copilot doing similar work inside their own apps; Claude doing it inside its own chat interface is Anthropic's answer to the same shift. If your team's workflow still involves drafting in Claude, copying into Google Docs or Word, then formatting a deck by hand, this is a real chance to cut a step out, provided you're on a plan that has the feature yet.
